About me

I’m Lesley Langdon: a wife, mother, self‑trained chef, weekend horticulturist, and lifelong learner pursuing an EdD in Curriculum & Instruction with a focus on Instructional Design & Technology. My professional journey has taken me from the fast‑paced world of restaurant management to the dynamic challenges of real estate, and finally, to the rewarding field of education. Each step has shaped my ability to lead, adapt, and create meaningful experiences for those I serve.

I began my career in restaurant management, rising to the position of general manager at Chili’s, where I managed one of the company’s culinary test locations and a management training site. I gained hands‑on experience in leadership, organizational strategy, and mentoring new managers, skills that influenced not only my team but also the broader network of restaurants we supported.

In real estate, I embraced the opportunity to connect with clients during some of the most significant moments of their lives. Whether helping buyers find their dream home or ensuring sellers received maximum value, I developed strong skills in negotiation, active listening, and customer‑focused solutions. This career also introduced me to the evolving role of technology, sparking a passion for learning and applying tech tools that later inspired my move into computer science.

Balancing my career and family responsibilities, I pursued a degree in computer science while continuing to work in real estate. This period taught me the value of resilience, adaptability, and prioritization. As my daughter’s needs evolved, I chose to focus on my education full‑time, earning a BS in computer science and beginning an MBA program.

In 2021, I began a new chapter as a computer science educator, combining my technical knowledge with my passion for helping others achieve their goals. I also founded The Technology Access Project (TAP), a nonprofit that teaches coding and robotics to kids in grades 4–8, where I design the curriculum, course materials, and programs. Now, as I move into instructional design, I bring a deep understanding of how to design learning experiences that are impactful, engaging, and adaptable to diverse audiences.

Thank you for visiting my site. Here you’ll find examples of my work, my mission, and ways to connect. I look forward to sharing my journey and continuing to help others succeed.

My mission

To encourage, guide, and support others on their path to success.

My mission applies to everyone I work with: family, clients, colleagues, direct reports, and students. In our home, we celebrate each other’s dreams, no matter how big or small. When my daughter shared that she wants to be a princess because “they help people,” I couldn’t have been prouder. Her response affirmed my belief that we all have the capacity to make a difference, no matter the role we play.

I developed this mission statement by reflecting on my career and identifying the common thread in every role I’ve held: helping others succeed. This guiding principle has shaped my leadership style, my teaching philosophy, and my approach to designing learning experiences. It’s also the reason I’m passionate about instructional design: creating opportunities for learners to grow and thrive is what drives me forward.

How my mission developed

In my career as a realtor, I found tremendous satisfaction in working with first‑time investors and helping people rebuild after difficult circumstances. Whether guiding an investor through a property flip or helping a family secure a rental after losing their home, I embraced my role as a trusted advisor. These experiences taught me the value of patience, diligence, and listening to understand others’ needs, skills I carry into my work today.

My journey in restaurant management was similarly centered on fostering success for others. As the general manager of a Chili’s management training location, I had the privilege of mentoring future leaders. Feedback from their supervisors consistently highlighted the strength of their preparation, which was a point of pride for me. I learned that leadership isn’t about shaping others to fit a mold; it’s about meeting people where they are and helping them achieve their unique goals. Whether developing a high‑performing employee into a trainer or mentoring an assistant manager eager for promotion, I strived to provide personalized support and clear pathways for growth.

One of the most rewarding moments in my restaurant career came when I spearheaded efforts to help my team find jobs after the closure of a restaurant I had worked in. By reaching out to local businesses and creating a hiring resource, I ensured that everyone who wanted a job found one before the doors closed. This experience underscored my belief in the power of proactive support and community, a belief that continues to shape my work today.

Now, as I move into instructional design, my mission remains at the core of everything I do. I aim to create learning experiences that are personalized, impactful, and empowering. Whether mentoring colleagues, designing educational programs, or developing technology‑driven solutions, I am driven by the opportunity to guide others toward their goals. Helping people succeed isn’t just part of my career; it’s who I am.
